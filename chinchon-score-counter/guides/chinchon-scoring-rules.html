Chinchón scoring rules: the −10, the −25 and card values

When a Chinchón round closes, every unmelded card adds its face value in penalty points (face cards count 10). Closing with everything melded subtracts 10 points; a chinchón made with a joker subtracts 25; and a chinchón without a joker wins the game outright.

That is the whole system. The fine print — tiebreakers, limits and the joker — is below.

Card values

Number cards score their number (ace is 1) and face cards score 10. The joker, if caught unmelded in your hand, typically costs 25 penalty points — a double-edged sword.

What scores and what does not

At the close of each round, melded combinations (sets of 3+ equal cards or runs of 3+ in one suit) score nothing. Everything left loose in your hand adds to your running total. In Chinchón, scoring is bad: the lowest total wins.

The bonuses

  • −10: for closing with all 7 cards melded.
  • −25: for a chinchón (7-card run in one suit) using a joker.
  • Instant win: a chinchón without a joker ends the game on the spot.

The limit and elimination

Games are played to an agreed limit, usually 70 or 100 points. Passing it eliminates you (or you re-enter with the highest surviving score, if your table allows re-entries). The last player under the limit wins — and takes the pot if there are stakes.
