Chinchón vs Conga vs Golpe: what actually changes?

Chinchón, Conga and Golpe are regional variants of the same card game: 7 cards, runs and sets, penalty points for unmelded cards. What changes is mostly the name by country, the conditions for closing, and a few scoring values.

If you can play one, you can play all three. Here is what shifts from table to table.

The shared skeleton

All three deal 7 cards, alternate draw-and-discard turns, meld combinations of at least 3 cards, and punish loose cards with penalty points when someone closes. Games run to a limit (70, 100…) with elimination.

Chinchón (Spain)

Named after the maximum play: a 7-card run in one suit, which wins the game outright without a joker. Typical bonuses: −10 for closing fully melded, −25 for a chinchón with a joker. Usually played with the ace of coins as joker.

Conga (Argentina and Uruguay)

The common name in the Río de la Plata region. Rules are essentially Spanish Chinchón; table details differ, like the exact value of the card you may close with or how the joker is handled. In many homes "conga" also names the fully-melded close.

Golpe

A variant where the close ("dar el golpe" — striking) has its own rules: some tables allow closing more aggressively without waiting for a low leftover card. Penalty scoring works the same.

The golden rule: agree first

Like any oral tradition, every table has its own version. Settle before dealing: what can you close with? What does a loose joker cost? Are re-entries allowed? Five seconds of agreement save twenty minutes of arguing.
