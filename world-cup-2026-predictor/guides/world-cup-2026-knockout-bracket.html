The World Cup 2026 knockout bracket, explained

The 2026 knockout stage starts with a new Round of 32: 24 group qualifiers plus the 8 best third-placed teams in a fixed single-elimination bracket. Five wins in that bracket — after three group games — make you world champion.

The bracket is not random: every slot is defined in advance by group position. Here is how the pairings work.

How the Round of 32 is paired

Each tie is written into the bracket by label: winners of certain groups face runners-up of others, and eight ties involve a third-placed team. Group winners are rewarded with (on paper) easier opponents, but with 8 thirds advancing, "easy" is relative.

Where do the third-placed teams go?

It depends on which groups produce the best thirds. FIFA's regulations include an allocation table covering all 495 possible combinations, assigning each qualified third to a specific slot so no team can meet a side from its own group before the final rounds.

The rounds after

Round of 16, quarter-finals, semi-finals, the third-place match and the final. From the Round of 32 onwards there are no second chances: draws go to extra time and penalties.
