Who will win the 2026 World Cup?

Nobody knows — and that is exactly why predictions are fun. The usual suspects top every list: the reigning champions, the top of the FIFA ranking, and the big European and South American squads. But a 48-team format with an extra knockout round means more chances for an upset than ever.

Instead of borrowing someone else's prediction, here is how to build your own — and put it to the test.

What history says

Only eight nations have ever won a World Cup, and every final since 2002 has featured a European or South American side. Home advantage matters too: hosts consistently overperform, which is worth considering with the tournament spread across the USA, Mexico and Canada.

What the format changes

A champion must now win 8 matches. The extra Round of 32 adds one more banana skin for favourites, and more rest-day variance across a bracket that spans three countries and dozens of venues. Deep squads matter more than ever.

How to make a prediction you can defend

Pick your champion, then work backwards: who do they beat in the final? Which semi-final do they survive? If you cannot name a plausible path through the bracket, your pick is a feeling, not a prediction. That is fine too — but the bracket exposes it.

Put your prediction to the test

In the World Cup 2026 Predictor app you can go quick with Champion Only mode, or commit to a full bracket with Groups + Knockouts and All Results. The app saves your predictions, and sharing them with friends turns the whole tournament into a competition of its own.

The only honest answer

The team that wins 8 matches. Everything else is opinion — so make yours count, in writing, before the next kickoff.
